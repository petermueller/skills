I did not understand that. Say it again, simply.

Restate your previous reply — or, if I give a target below, that target — in ASD-STE100 Simplified Technical English. Talk to me the way one engineer talks to another across a desk: plain, direct, and friendly. Do not write a manual page.

Target (empty means your previous reply): $ARGUMENTS

## What to keep

- Keep every fact, decision, number, warning and next step that matters. Do not add new information or new opinions.
- Keep code identifiers, file paths, commands, error text and product names exactly as they are. Put them in backticks. They are technical names, so STE allows them.
- If the original was vague or skipped a step, say so in one short sentence. Do not guess to fill the gap.
- Lead with the main point: what happened, or what I must know or do. Then give the reasons and details.

## STE rules to follow

- Short sentences: 20 words max for an instruction, 25 words max for a description.
- One idea per sentence. One topic per paragraph. Six sentences max per paragraph.
- Active voice. Say who or what does the action ("The script deletes the file", not "The file is deleted").
- Simple tenses only: present, simple past, simple future.
- Write instructions as commands: "Run the tests." Put a warning or condition before the action it applies to.
- No `-ing` verb forms, except inside technical names.
- Use simple, common words, each with one meaning. Use the same word for the same thing every time. Do not use a synonym for variety.
- Keep "the", "a" and "this". Do not drop articles to save words.
- Break up noun clusters longer than three words: "the cache invalidation retry timeout" becomes "the timeout for retries when the cache is cleared".
- No idioms, slang, metaphors or phrasal verbs that have a simple one-word equivalent ("kick off" → "start", "figure out" → "find").
- No contractions.

## Shape of the answer

- Mostly short paragraphs, like speech. Use a numbered list only for steps that must happen in order, and a bulleted list only for 3 or more parallel items.
- No headings, no tables, no "Summary:" label, no preamble such as "Sure, here it is". Start with the content.
- Be shorter than the original if you can, but never drop a fact to get there.
- End with the one action I must do next, if there is one.

Do not call tools, and do not do new work. Only restate.
